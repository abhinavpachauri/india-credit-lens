August’s RBI data is out for both credit and payments, two key insights :

1. Big companies are slowing down. Smaller ones are not.

Two years ago (Dec 2024), big companies got 71 of every ₹100 lent to industry. Now it’s 66.5. And August alone knocked off almost a full point (67.3 → 66.5).

Meanwhile, loans to mid-sized companies are growing 31.8% a year. That’s more than double the big guys. Small businesses continues to show healthy growth of 23%.

1a. So the new money is going to Services, and mostly NBFCs.

Of every ₹100 of new bank credit this year, ₹35 went to services, though services was only ₹27 of every ₹100 a year ago.

Bank loans to NBFCs are up 37.5%. Banks are basically lending to lenders, who then lend to you and me. More on NBFC breakup in further posts as RBI has started to release a separate breakup of NBFC recently.


2. On the payments side: we’re swiping more, but spending smaller.

Credit card swipes at shops: up 23.5% in a year. Rupee value: up only 9.1%.
Online card transactions: up 32.2%. Value: up only 3.9%.

More swipes, smaller bills. The credit card looks less like a “big purchase” card now and more like an everyday one: groceries, fuel, food orders.

And there are now 12.4 crore credit cards in India, growing 10.3%, the fastest in our data.
