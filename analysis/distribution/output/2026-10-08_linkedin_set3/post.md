August’s RBI data, part 3: what happens when you take inflation out?

A loan book growing 15% sounds great. But if prices in that business went up 38%, the business is actually borrowing less. So I put RBI’s credit numbers next to MoSPI’s price and output data. Three things stood out.

1. Inflation flatters some loan books and hides others.

Petroleum: bank credit is up 14.8% in rupees. But oil prices (WPI mineral oils) are up 38.5%, so in real terms credit is down 17.1%. Production? Flat, 0.0%. And in August alone, yearly credit growth dropped from 34.0% to 14.8%.

Gems & jewellery: up 39.1% on paper. Take out jewellery prices (+23.6%) and it’s 12.6%. Still growing, just a lot less than it looks.

Power is the opposite. Credit is up 21.0%, and since electricity prices actually fell (−1.7%), real growth is even higher: 23.1%. Power output grew only 5.2%. Lending is running way ahead of generation.

2. Loans are growing about twice as fast as the economy.

In the April–June quarter, after inflation:
Services: credit +18.6%, output +10.2%
Industry: credit +14.8%, output +7.9%
Agriculture: credit +12.6%, output +3.1%. That’s 4x.

Is that India formalising and moving to bank finance, or more debt per rupee of output? Honestly, both can be true. In August, though, industry credit cooled (20.0% → 18.2% growth in one month) while services sped up (22.9% → 24.3%).

3. Credit cards: we swipe more, but owe less.

12.4 crore cards (+10.3%), shop spending +9.1%. But card debt outstanding grew just 3.6%. After inflation (CPI +4.8%), that’s −1.1%. Real card debt has shrunk in 6 of the 8 months of 2026.

More people seem to be paying their bill in full. Good for card safety, not so good for bank profits. But August had the smallest fall since May (−2.1% → −1.1%).

Data: RBI sector-wise bank credit + ATM/POS card statistics; MoSPI WPI, CPI, IIP and national accounts · Aug 2026 · indiacreditlens.com
