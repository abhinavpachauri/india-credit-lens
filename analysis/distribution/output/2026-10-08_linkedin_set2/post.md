August’s RBI data, part 2: who’s winning market share? Three shifts worth watching

1. Credit cards: the big banks are growing, just slower than the market.

India’s credit cards grew 10.3% in a year. The four biggest issuers all grew slower: HDFC 8.3%, SBI 7.6%, ICICI 9.1%, Axis 6.4%. So they’re slowly giving up share, even while adding lakhs of cards.

Who’s taking it? Federal Bank’s cards are up 81.8%, IDFC First’s 25.2%. Small finance banks more than doubled (+111%), though they’re still only 2% of the market.

But a card in your wallet isn’t a card you use. On actual spending, it’s the government banks. Public sector banks had 19.4% of card spending at shops a year ago, but took 51% of this year’s growth. SBI’s shop spend growth jumped from 16.2% to 22.3% in just one month. HDFC, the biggest, grew shop spend by only 0.9%.

2. Payments: two banks are behind almost every UPI QR in India.

Of the 81.3 crore UPI QR codes out there, Yes Bank has 77.4% and Axis 16%. That’s roughly 93 of every 100 QR stickers you scan.


3. Personal loans: from homes to gold.

Home loans are almost half of all personal loans (47.4%) but took only a third of this year’s growth (32.8%), growing just 11.1%. Gold loans are only 7.7% of personal loans, yet took 24.1% of the growth, up 83.2% in a year. (Part of that is simply gold getting pricier.)

New in August: loans against fixed deposits. Their yearly growth jumped from 14.6% to 43.2% in one month. Could NRI deposits (FCNR) be behind this?
