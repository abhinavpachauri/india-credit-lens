## 1. What the source actually is (read, not assumed)

Three files in the repo root, one per monthly release:

| file | released | data month |
|---|---|---|
| `NBFCSDC07072026.xlsx` | 7 Jul 2026 | May 2026 |
| `NBFCSDCE07082026_AN.xlsx` | 7 Aug 2026 | Jun 2026 |
| `NBFCCREDIT07092026_A.xlsx` | 7 Sep 2026 | Jul 2026 |

One sheet (`Press Release`), 15 sector rows, ₹ crore. **Each release carries FIVE dated
columns, not one**: `M-24`, the prior-prior FY end, `M-12`, the prior FY end, `M`.

**So "three months of data" is really eleven dates**, and seven of them can compute a YoY
on day one:

```
2024-05-31  2024-06-30  2024-07-31                       level only (no 2023 in any file)
2025-03-31                                               level only (no 2024-03)
2025-05-31  2025-06-30  2025-07-31   ← YoY vs 2024
2026-03-31                           ← YoY vs 2025-03
2026-05-31  2026-06-30  2026-07-31   ← YoY vs 2025
```

This matters: it is the difference between shipping a dashboard dimension with a flat line
and shipping one with a real series. **SIBC's own lesson applies in reverse** — signals
exist per ingested PERIOD, and SIBC spent a separate session backfilling 11 → 24. NBFC
should register all eleven from the first run, with the four level-only dates registered
**level-only and saying so**, exactly as the SIBC 2024 backfill did.

### 1.1 The hierarchy — and the one thing that is different from SIBC

```
NBFC Credit                                    total, ₹59.89 L Cr @ Jul 2026, +14.94% YoY
├─ 1. Agriculture and Allied Activities        ₹0.80 L Cr   +17.97%
├─ 2. Industry                    of which     ₹22.13 L Cr   +7.38%
│   └─ 2.1 Infrastructure         of which     ₹20.16 L Cr   +6.24%
│       └─ 2.1.1 Power                         ₹14.36 L Cr   +6.89%
├─ 3. Services                    of which     ₹7.69 L Cr   +15.19%
│   ├─ 3.1 Transport Operators                 ₹1.79 L Cr   +10.65%
│   ├─ 3.2 Trade                               ₹1.16 L Cr   +14.65%
│   └─ 3.3 Commercial Real Estate              ₹1.03 L Cr   +22.34%
├─ 4. Retail Loans                of which     ₹26.06 L Cr   +21.43%
│   ├─ 4.1 Housing Loans*                      ₹8.55 L Cr   +11.87%
│   ├─ 4.2 Vehicle Loans                       ₹6.29 L Cr   +15.09%
│   ├─ 4.3 Loans against gold jewellery        ₹3.54 L Cr   +68.52%   ← the headline
│   └─ 4.4 Consumer Durables                   ₹0.75 L Cr   +51.46%
└─ 5. Other Loans**                            ₹3.21 L Cr   +19.69%
```

**Level 1 is an exact partition** — the five sectors sum to the total to the rupee at all
eleven dates (verified, diff 0.0). **Levels 2 and 3 are "of which" subsets**: Services'
three named children cover 52% of Services, Retail's four cover 73%, Industry's one covers
91%. Nothing declares the remainder.

That single fact drives most of §3, because the movement family's `alloc` divides by the
**sum of the parts**, and for an "of which" cut that denominator is not the cut's movement.
This is the identical defect already recorded as open on PSL in `CLAUDE.local.md`
("PSL's `weight`/`weight_now` divide by the sum of those same non-additive parts").
**NBFC is the case that forces it to be fixed properly rather than carried.**

### 1.2 Two gifts this source gives that neither existing pipeline has

1. **RBI publishes the YoY itself** (columns J and K). Our computed YoY can be gated
   against the source's own figure — free external ground truth, checked to 1e-4 by hand on
   the total (14.94207 published vs 14.94207 computed). No other pipeline has this. It
   becomes a gate stage, not a spot check.
2. **The press release states the headline in machine-readable text**, per the 2026-09-11
   delivery note — a second independent anchor for the extractor.

### 1.3 Three caveats that must be written into the spec, not discovered later

- **Sample, not census.** Footnote 2: Upper- and Middle-Layer NBFCs + HFCs, **~87% of
  total NBFC credit** per RTP 2024-25. Every cross-pipeline ratio in §5 is therefore a
  ratio against 87% of the book. It must be stated wherever the number is published — this
  is a `gap_` node in the system model, authored on day one, the same way
  `gap_psl_totals_methodology` carries PSL's non-additivity.
- **Revisions are real but tiny.** `2026-03-31` reads 5,775,989.3656 / .3760 / .3661 across
  the three releases — float noise, not restatement. Policy is still SIBC's:
  **last release wins, re-append every period, freshness proves it.** Do not special-case.
- **The store will have gaps until releases accumulate.** The prior period of `2025-05-31`
  is `2025-03-31` — two months, not one. Any rule reading `prev_value` (status rules,
  acceleration) would silently compare across a gap. **Make the gap loud**: a prior-period
  comparison computes only when the gap is one cadence step, else the row is absent with a
  declared reason. This is `feedback_failure_that_looks_like_absence` applied before the
  fact instead of after it.

## 2. Reuse — what NBFC inherits, and the one refactor that decides the rest

`ARCHITECTURE.md` §"Adding a pipeline" already counted this. NBFC inherits the gate, the
cut tables (discovered from the registry), the state band (derived), `table_rows`, every
validator and the cadence vocabulary **for free**. What it does not inherit:

| gap | the cheap way | the right way | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| compute module | copy `compute/sibc.py` (960 LOC) → `compute/nbfc.py` | make it pipeline-parameterised | **right way — see below** |
| card generator | copy 753 LOC | no third generator; table + band only | **no generator (D2)** |
| web adapter + route | copy `SibcReadMode` (211 LOC) | copy, then generalise later | copy for v1 |
| `PIPELINE_IDS` literal | add `"nbfc"` in 15 places | read from the manifest | **read from manifest (D1)** |

### 2.1 The compute module is a five-line problem, not a 960-line one

I expected the copy to be forced. It is not. `compute/sibc.py` binds to its pipeline in
exactly **three module-level constants**:

```python
CSV      = manifest.consolidated_csv("sibc")     # line 50
TIMELINE = REPO / "analysis" / "rbi_sibc" / "timeline.json"
_df_cache: pd.DataFrame | None = None
```

Everything else is already generic: `statement` is a **compute param with a default**
(`params.get("statement", "Statement 1")`), `is_psl` likewise. Seventeen methods, none of
which know what pipeline they serve.

**So the proper fix is small:** `compute/sibc.py` → `compute/csv_sector.py`, taking
`pipeline` through `compute(...)`, with a per-pipeline df cache and the CSV read from the
manifest. SIBC keeps a thin binding so no registry entry, no method name and no test import
changes. NBFC declares in its manifest that it has no `statement` column and no PSL flag,
and the `_val` filter skips what is not declared.

**The proof is free and already built.** `check_signal_freshness` recomputes every SIBC
period from the CSV and fails on any drift — 17,809 SIBC rows must come back
byte-identical through the refactor, which is exactly how the `csv_streak` split and the
`one cut, one name` rename were proven no-ops. If it is not a no-op, we find out in 25
seconds.

This closes item 2 of `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s list, with NBFC as the case that tests it —
which is what that section asked for.

## 3. Signal spec (the part to get right before any compute runs)

`analysis/signals/README.md` gains an NBFC section; the registry gains the entries below.
**Nothing is computed until the spec is written**, per the standing spec-first rule.

### 3.1 Layer 1 — what the cuts are

Five cuts. Naming follows `one cut, one name` (`nbfc-{slug}-{family}`):

| stem | subject | parts | additive? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nbfc-main` | NBFC Credit | the 5 level-1 sectors | **yes** (exact) |
| `nbfc-industry` | Industry | Infrastructure | no — "of which" |
| `nbfc-infra` | Infrastructure | Power | no — "of which" |
| `nbfc-services` | Services | Transport / Trade / CRE | no — "of which" |
| `nbfc-retail` | Retail Loans | Housing / Vehicle / Gold / Consumer Durables | no — "of which" |

Per cut, the families §17–§20 made the generic L1 surface — **size, share-of-parent,
share-of-book, growth, momentum, acceleration, allocation** — plus the cut's own parent
rate. Every entry declares `cadence: "monthly"` and `window: 12` (both now required
fields; Check 2e fails a signal without cadence).

Plus scalars the prose layer needs: `nbfc-credit-abs`, `nbfc-credit-yoy`,
`nbfc-retail-yoy`, `nbfc-gold-yoy`, and the FY-end pair (`2025-03-31` / `2026-03-31` are
both in the store, so `csv_sector_fy_delta` works from day one).

Estimated registry growth: **~55 entries**, 527 → ~582.

### 3.2 The denominator rule — MEASURED per cut, never declared (D3, settled)

**What `allocation` is.** For a cut it answers *"of the new money that went into this book
over 12 months, what share did each part take?"* It needs a denominator: the total movement
of the thing being decomposed. Today the compute uses **the sum of the parts' movements**,
which is right exactly when the parts ARE the whole.

**The rule (user's, 2026-09-16): compute whether they are. Don't declare it.** A
hand-written `additive: true|false` field is another `MOVEMENT_CUTS` — a second description
of the data that agrees today and drifts later. Instead, per cut per period:

```
        Σ parts  ==  parent's published row  ?
              │                        │
             yes                       no ────────── parent row published?
              │                                   │              │
    denominator = Σ parts                        yes             no
    (identical to the parent —                    │              │
     use either)                    denominator = parent's    WITHHOLD
                                    published net, and         and declare
                                    state the coverage         the reason
```

One logic, every cut, both pipelines.

### 3.2.1 What it measures — enumerated over all 12 SIBC cuts × all 24 dates

Not sampled. The separation is absolute, so no threshold needs tuning:

```
ADDITIVE cuts — worst residual across every cut, every date: 0.000000%
NON-additive:  sibc-main       4.5% – 5.6%   (24 of 24 dates)
               sibc-nbfc-sub  63.9% – 67.2%  (24 of 24 dates)
```

Ten of twelve cuts are **exactly** additive — the rule is a provable no-op there. Two change,
and the movement gap (what allocation actually divides by) at 2026-07-31 is:

| cut | parent net | Σ parts net | today's shares are | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sibc-main` | +3,515,561 | +3,351,049 | **×1.049 too high** | RBI attributes ₹10.7 L Cr to no sector |
| `sibc-nbfc-sub` | +559,147 | +223,995 | **×2.49 too high** | HFCs + PFIs are 34.7% of bank credit to NBFCs |
| `sibc-psl` | *none published* | — | **withheld** | overlapping memo lenses, §3.2.2 |
| the other 9 | — | — | unchanged | |

**The most-broken cut today is `sibc-nbfc-sub`** — the exact entity this new pipeline hangs
off. A card there saying "HFCs took X% of new NBFC credit" is overstating by a factor of
2.5, because two named children cover a third of the parent. Fixing it is a prerequisite
for the §5 cross-source work being trustworthy, not a side quest.

⚠️ **The trap, and it is the `feedback_check_population` shape again.** `sibc-industry-type`
*looks* parentless: its parts are Statement 2, its total is Statement 1, and **Statement 2
carries no code "2" at all**. Its 19 parts sum to ₹4,800,777 — the published Industry total,
**exactly**. So it is branch 1, and a naive implementation would misread "I cannot find the
parent" as "there is no parent" and withhold a correct allocation. Therefore: **a parent
code that is declared but not found must RAISE, never silently withhold.** The share scan
already solved this with `parent_statement`; momentum and allocation must declare it too.

### 3.2.2 PSL is the withhold branch — and its invented denominator is live today

**RBI publishes no PSL total.** PSL's ten lines are overlapping lenses over the main tree,
not a partition: PSL "Micro and Small Enterprises" (₹30.88 L Cr) spans industry *and*
services and is not the Statement-1 ₹10.92 L Cr row; a weaker-section loan to an MSE
borrower is counted in **both** that line and "Weaker Sections" (₹21.22 L Cr). Their sum is
not a book, so a share of it is not a share of anything.

The dashboard says both things at once, in one block:

```
no_speed_note : "Priority sector is a memo lens over the main tree, not a slice of it,
                 so RBI publishes no total for it to grow at."
mix           : "Drifting toward Micro and Small Enterprises — it took 37.2% of the
                 growth while holding 30.0% of the total a year ago."
```

The second sentence divides by the total the first says does not exist. Under the rule PSL
lands in the withhold branch: `alloc`, `weight`, `weight_now` go, the derived mix state with
them, and the band's mix row carries its declared reason — the designed behaviour for a
reading that cannot exist. Size, share-of-book, growth and momentum are untouched.
Precedent is already set: `share-of-parent` was deleted for PSL rather than given an
invented total (`gap_psl_totals_methodology`).

### 3.2.3 What it means for NBFC

`nbfc-main` is branch 1 (the five sectors sum to the total to the rupee at all eleven
dates). The four "of which" cuts are branch 2 — the parent row is always published, so the
correct denominator always exists. Worked example, NBFC Services Jul 2025 → Jul 2026:

```
                       over Σ PARTS   over PARENT's net
Transport Operators          33.8%           17.0%
Trade                        29.0%           14.6%
Commercial Real Estate       37.2%           18.6%
unnamed remainder               —            49.8%
```

×1.99 across the board: the card would read *"CRE took 37% of all new NBFC services
credit"* when it took **18.6%**, because RBI names 51.7% of that cut and half the new money
went somewhere it does not break out.

### 3.3 Layer 2

- **Skeleton only, on day one.** `generate_skeleton.py` is deterministic and gives the 15
  entity nodes, the structural edges, URNs and `concept_tags` for free. Mix states fall out
  of the movement rows with no new registry entries (that mechanism is already keyed on
  `compute.parent_code`), so the state band lights up on all five cuts with zero authoring.
- **Behavioral layer (forces/channels) DEFERRED.** A FOUNDATION pass wants a series long
  enough to argue over; eleven dates with two-month gaps is not it, and a force authored
  now would be sourced against a window we cannot yet measure. Revisit at ~6 releases
  (≈ Feb 2027), or at the first FY-end file. **`_meta.update_note` records the deferral
  explicitly** so the absence is declared rather than looking like a miss.
- The one exception worth authoring immediately is the `gap_` node for the 87% sample
  (§1.3) — it is a fact about the source, not a causal claim, and everything downstream
  needs to be able to cite it.

### 3.4 Layer 3

Out of scope. It consumes L2a/L2b causal graphs, and NBFC has none yet.

## 5. The SIBC ↔ NBFC relationship (the third ask)

This is the strongest cross-source pairing the platform has had. SIBC↔payments shares a
handful of concepts; **SIBC and NBFC share a taxonomy.**

### 5.1 Two distinct relationships — they must not be conflated

**(a) The vertical one — funding → deployment.** SIBC's `3.9 Non-Banking Financial
Companies` (₹21.28 L Cr @ Jul 2026) is banks' *claim on* NBFCs. The NBFC pipeline is what
NBFCs did with their money. Bank credit is **35.5%** of the NBFC book — a funding-dependence
proxy, not an identity (bonds, ECB, deposits and equity fund the rest, and the book is the
87% sample). Modelled as a **reconciliation constraint** with a plausibility corridor,
exactly like `cx_cc_balance_per_card`: a move outside the corridor means a definition or
roster break, not an economic event.

**(b) The horizontal one — the same borrower, two channels.** Eight sectors appear in both
statements. This is where the analysis actually lives:

```
sector                bank ₹Cr     NBFC ₹Cr    NBFC share of the two
Power                   869,333   1,436,271          62.3%    ← NBFCs are the majority lender
Consumer Durables        23,027      74,644          76.4%    ← banks have almost left
Vehicle Loans           764,554     629,239          45.1%
Gold jewellery          552,261     353,988          39.1%    ← NBFC side +68.5% YoY
Transport Operators     290,424     178,772          38.1%
Housing                3,428,453    854,627          20.0%
Commercial Real Est.    669,260     103,485          13.4%
Trade                 1,408,848     115,516           7.6%
```

The platform has never been able to say *"banks hold 38% of India's power credit"*. It can
now — and the same eight rows answer "where are banks retreating and NBFCs advancing",
which is a genuine credit-cycle read, not a restatement.

### 5.2 How it is modelled — reusing the hub, adding nothing new

Everything needed exists in `COMPOSITION_SPEC` Part II:

1. **`ontology/concepts.json`** gains the shared concept tags (`power`, `vehicle_loan`,
   `gold_loan`, `consumer_durable`, `housing`, `cre`, `transport`, `trade`). NBFC entities
   carry URNs (`icl:nbfc/2.1.1`) and those tags; SIBC entities already carry most.
2. **`derive_cross_links.py`** then proposes the eight pairs on the existing shared-concept
   rule, with **no new derivation mechanism** — they land in `cross_source/candidates.json`
   for confirmation into `composition.json`. *If it does not propose them, the finding is in
   the deriver, and that is worth knowing.*
3. **One construct per shared sector** (`system_credit_to_power`), members = the two
   entities, direction + **coherence** computed by the existing `construct_direction`. The
   interesting state is the *contested* one: banks shrinking while NBFCs grow is precisely
   what coherence was built to route.
4. **One reconciliation constraint** `cx_nbfc_bank_funding_share` for 5.1(a), corridor
   authored wide (say 25–50%) and **negative-tested**, like `cx_cc_balance_per_card`.
5. **One cross-pipeline loop** (deferred with the rest of the behavioral layer): bank credit
   to NBFCs → NBFC deployment → asset growth → further bank funding.

### 5.3 How it reaches a reader — UI

⚠️ **CLAUDE.md authoring rule: ASCII layout first, explicit approval, then code.** So this
section states the *shape* and nothing is built until an ASCII pass is approved separately.

- **v1, no new surface.** The eight pairs are cross-system items; the read mode already
  renders cross-system findings inline (`crossSystemFor()`), matched to a dimension. A
  "banks hold 38% of power credit" card attaches to the SIBC Industry dimension **and** the
  NBFC Industry dimension from one computed item.
- **v2, the surface this data earns — a channel column.** The §17 table is per-cut rows ×
  declared columns. A shared-sector cut could carry `bank ₹ | NBFC ₹ | NBFC share | Δ share`
  using `table_rows` unchanged, because a column is declared per cut. That is the cheapest
  possible route to the finding and it reuses the generic surface rather than adding one.
- **Deliberately NOT v1:** a combined "system credit" total. Adding bank and NBFC books
  double-counts — bank credit to NBFCs funds the NBFC book. Publishing a sum would be
  wrong, and `nbfc-double-counting` is already a registry entry saying so.

## 8. Risks

- **Phase 1 is the one that can bite.** A 960-LOC module serving a live pipeline; mitigated
  entirely by freshness, which recomputes every row in ~25s. If it is not a no-op, stop.
- **Format drift.** Three files, one format. The detector is being written against n=3; the
  fourth release is the real test. Keep stage 0 loud rather than tolerant.
- **The 87% sample invites a wrong sentence** ("NBFC credit is ₹59.9 L Cr" — it is not,
  that is 87% of it). This is a prose risk, so it belongs to gate 5.8 and the `gap_` node,
  not to a compute check.
- **Eight cross-links are eight chances to pair the wrong entities.** Bank "Housing" is
  `4.2 Housing (incl. Priority Sector Housing)`; NBFC "Housing Loans*" includes HFC lending,
  and HFCs also appear inside SIBC as `3.9.1`. Every pair gets a written note on what the
  two sides do and do not cover, or it does not get confirmed.
